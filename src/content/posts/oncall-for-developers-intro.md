Engineers being on call is a very controversial concept in the industry. This makes sense because quite frankly there are some horrific on call practices out there. I've had the priviledge of experiencing a variety of on call patterns as a software engineer and would like to share some thoughts and opinions I've developed over the years in a series of blog posts.

## Who is this for?
There are many resources out there for SRE teams dedicated to maintaining service health, however I find there are not too many resources for software engineers who are expected to be on call and support the systems they developed. I have learned so much both about on call practices and personal tips for being successful and would like to pay it forward to others who may either be nervous about joining an on call rotation or trying to build up healthy on call practices on their teams.

## What will be covered?
Over a series of posts I plan to cover:

- Options for on call setups
- Playbooks, monitoring, and alerting
- How to be effective in a crisis scenario
- Practices for healthy rotations

I think the human element of on call is always the most important and is what I want to focus on. I've had experiences where that was at the forefront and others where it was an afterthought. The ones that prioritized the wellbeing of the team were always the ones that were the most sustainable and kept engineers around longer (surprise, surprise!).

## What will not be covered
I don't plan on covering more technical details of service reliability. If you are interested in that I highly recommend reading [the Google SRE Books](https://sre.google/books/) as it is probably the most comprehensive (and free) overview of how to build and maintain services that are maintainable.
